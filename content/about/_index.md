I am a **quantitative ecologist** interested in understanding biodiversity patterns and change across spatial and temporal scales. My research combines **plant ecology, ethnobotany, functional diversity, biodiversity monitoring and ecological modelling**, with a particular interest in the relationships between people, plants and ecosystems.

During my PhD, I studied floristic, functional and cultural diversity across western Amazonian forests, exploring how plant traits, species composition and ecological gradients relate to traditional plant uses and Indigenous ecological knowledge. My work involved extensive botanical and ethnobotanical fieldwork in Peru, tropical plant identification and collaboration with international research networks.

I am currently a **postdoctoral researcher at the Estación Biológica de Doñana (EBD-CSIC)**, working within the European **ANTENNA** project. My research focuses on biodiversity monitoring, ecological time series, population trends and uncertainty, with the aim of improving the reliability of biodiversity assessments and ecological forecasting.

I am also interested in reproducible research, open science and developing computational tools that make ecological research more accessible.

## Research interests

- **Tropical ecology and biogeography** — Floristic diversity, forest composition and patterns of species dominance.
- **Ethnobotany and biocultural diversity** — Traditional plant uses, Indigenous ecological knowledge and human–plant relationships.
- **Functional ecology** — Plant functional traits and their links to ecosystem functioning and benefits to people.
- **Biodiversity change** — Population trends, ecological monitoring, time-series uncertainty and forecasting.
- **Ecoinformatics** — Ecological data analysis, reproducible workflows and scientific software development.
